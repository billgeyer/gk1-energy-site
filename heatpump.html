Making it affordable

Switching costs real money up front. There are ways to ease that.

A rebate, a 0% loan, and installer financing can each take pressure off the up-front cost, so you do not have to start with a large check. Figures here come from Mass Save or your utility. Last checked October 9, 2026.

Mass Save heat pump rebates (2026)

Mass Save pays a rebate on qualifying heat pumps: up to $8,500 for a whole-home system in 2026. What you get depends on your system and your home. Mass Save also lists offers based on income.

Its page has the current amounts and rules, and it is the place to check them.

0% HEAT Loan

Mass Save offers a 0% HEAT Loan of up to $25,000. The length of the loan is set by the lender. For a whole-home heat pump, the loan needs a Home Energy Assessment first.

Installer financing

Some installers offer their own financing, including plans where you pay over time instead of up front. Those terms are set by the company offering them, not by Mass Save, so get them in writing.

In the Check-Up I go through the real terms with you and compare them with the rebate and the loan.

A lower winter electric rate

Massachusetts electric utilities offer a lower winter rate for homes that heat with a heat pump. Mass Save describes it as running from November through April. Which rate applies depends on who supplies your electricity, and the details differ by utility.

A home heated only with electric baseboards does not get the rate until it has a heat pump.

I do not quote a dollar figure here, because the utility's numbers change. At the Check-Up I work out what it would mean for your house.

Common questions

Questions people ask me.

Short answers, each from Mass Save or your utility. For your own house, the Check-Up is where I get specific.

Will a heat pump keep my house warm in a New England winter?

Mass Save only pays its rebate on ENERGY STAR cold-climate models that are on its qualified list, installed by a contractor in its Heat Pump Installer Network. Whether one is enough for your house, or whether you would keep a backup, is something I look at in the Check-Up.

Mass Save: air-source heat pump rebates →

What does it cost, and what does Mass Save pay?

It depends on your house. Mass Save's page has its current rebate amounts and an example install. The Check-Up gives you the numbers for your house, not an example.

Can I keep my oil system as a backup?

Yes. Mass Save has a partial-home rebate for homes that keep their existing system as a backup, and it needs an integrated control. The whole-home rebate is for replacing oil, propane, or electric resistance heat.

Do I need insulation or other work first?

Possibly. For the whole-home rebate, the home has to be weatherized: built in 2000 or later, or a Mass Save Home Energy Assessment recommends a small amount of work and that work is done. Many older homes need the assessment first. Mass Save's page has the exact rule.

Will my electric bill go up?

Your electric use goes up, because the heat pump runs on electricity. That is why utilities offer a lower winter rate to homes with a heat pump. The rate lowers the price of the electricity. It is not the whole picture, so I compare your actual numbers in the Check-Up.

How a home gets onto the rate differs by utility. Your utility's page explains it, and I can check yours with you.

How many heat pumps would I need?

It depends on the size and layout of your house and how it is heated today. That is one of the things the Check-Up works out.

What is the 0% HEAT Loan, and how does it work?

Mass Save's HEAT Loan is 0% interest, up to $25,000. You have to be the homeowner, and the steps include a signed contractor proposal, an authorization form, lender approval, and a verification after the install. A whole-home heat pump needs a Home Energy Assessment first. The loan's length is set by the participating lender.

Mass Save: financing →

What about the federal tax credit?

It ended. Mass Save's 2025 page said the credit would no longer apply to work done after December 31, 2025. I mention it because you may still see older pages that list it.

Mass Save's 2025 page (archived September 9, 2025) →

How long does it take, and when do I get the rebate?

For 2026 rebates, the install has to be done by December 31, 2026, and the paperwork in by February 28, 2027. Mass Save asks you to allow about 8 weeks for processing.

Who installs it?

I work with qualified Mass Save installers to complete your project. For the Mass Save rebate, the installer has to be in Mass Save's Heat Pump Installer Network, and the equipment has to be on its qualified list. Ask any installer to confirm both before you sign.
