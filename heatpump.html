What helps with the cost

Switching costs real money up front, but there are programs that help.

Every figure here comes from Mass Save or your utility. Rebate amounts are maximums. What you get depends on your system and home. Last checked October 9, 2026.

Mass Save heat pump rebates (2026)

Whole-home: $2,650 per ton, up to $8,500, when a heat pump replaces oil, propane, or electric resistance heat. The home has to be weatherized: built in 2000 or later, or an assessment recommends under $1,000 of work.

Partial-home: $1,125 per ton, up to $8,500, when you keep your existing system as a backup. An integrated control is required.

Income-based: up to $16,000 or no cost. See Mass Save's page for who qualifies.

Mass Save's own example: a $22,000 install, an $8,500 rebate, and $13,500 net.

For 2026, the heat pump has to be installed by December 31, 2026, with paperwork in by February 28, 2027.

What changed since 2025

Mass Save's top whole-home rebate dropped from $10,000 in 2025 ($3,000 per ton) to $8,500 in 2026 ($2,650 per ton). The partial-home rebate went from $1,250 per ton, capped at $10,000, to $1,125 per ton, capped at $8,500.

The federal tax credit for heat pumps ended for work done after December 31, 2025. Mass Save's 2025 page had listed up to $2,000.

0% HEAT Loan

Mass Save offers a 0% HEAT Loan of up to $25,000. The length of the loan is set by the lender. For a whole-home heat pump, the loan needs a Home Energy Assessment first.

There are other ways to finance it too, including lease-style plans, so a big check up front is not the only path.

Mass Save: financing →

A lower winter electric rate

National Grid offers a lower winter delivery rate, November 1 through April 30, for homes that use a heat pump to heat. Eversource and Unitil have their own winter heat pump rates. If your electric bill comes from National Grid, this is the one that applies to you.

A home heated only with electric baseboards does not get the rate until it has a heat pump.

I do not quote a dollar figure here, because the utility's numbers change. At the Check-Up I work out what it would mean for your house.

Common questions

Questions people ask me.

Short answers, each from Mass Save or your utility. For your own house, the Check-Up is where I get specific.

Will a heat pump keep my house warm in a New England winter?

Mass Save only pays its rebate on ENERGY STAR cold-climate models that are on its qualified list, installed by a contractor in its Heat Pump Installer Network. Whether one is enough for your house, or whether you would keep a backup, is something I look at in the Check-Up.

Mass Save: air-source heat pump rebates →

What does it cost, and what does Mass Save pay?

It depends on your house. Mass Save's own example: a $22,000 install, an $8,500 rebate, and $13,500 net. The whole-home rebate is $2,650 per ton, up to $8,500. The Check-Up gives you the numbers for your house, not an example.

Can I keep my oil system as a backup?

Yes. Mass Save's partial-home rebate is for homes that keep their existing system as a backup. It pays $1,125 per ton, up to $8,500, and needs an integrated control. The whole-home rebate is for replacing oil, propane, or electric resistance heat.

Do I need insulation or other work first?

Possibly. For the whole-home rebate, the home has to be weatherized: built in 2000 or later, or a Mass Save Home Energy Assessment recommends under $1,000 of work, or that work is done. Many older homes need the assessment first.

Will my electric bill go up?

Your electric use goes up, because the heat pump runs on electricity. That is why utilities offer a lower winter rate, November 1 through April 30, to homes with a heat pump. The rate lowers the price of the electricity. It is not the whole picture, so I compare your actual numbers in the Check-Up.

National Grid enrolls homes with a Mass Save-supported heat pump automatically, usually 6 to 8 weeks after the rebate is paid.

National Grid: heat pump rate →

How many heat pumps would I need?

It depends on the size and layout of your house and how it is heated today. That is one of the things the Check-Up works out.

What is the 0% HEAT Loan, and how does it work?

Mass Save's HEAT Loan is 0% interest, up to $25,000. You have to be the homeowner, and the steps include a signed contractor proposal, an authorization form, lender approval, and a verification after the install. A whole-home heat pump needs a Home Energy Assessment first. The loan's length is set by the participating lender.

Mass Save: financing →

What about the federal tax credit?

It ended. Mass Save's 2025 page said the credit would no longer apply to work done after December 31, 2025. I mention it because you may still see older pages that list it.

How long does it take, and when do I get the rebate?

For 2026 rebates, the install has to be done by December 31, 2026, and the paperwork in by February 28, 2027. Mass Save asks you to allow about 8 weeks for processing.

Do I qualify for income-based help?

Mass Save lists an income-based rebate of up to $16,000, or no cost. I can't tell you from here whether you qualify. Mass Save's page explains who does, and we can go through it together at the Check-Up.

Who installs it?

For the Mass Save rebate, the installer has to be in Mass Save's Heat Pump Installer Network, and the equipment has to be on its qualified list. Ask any installer to confirm both before you sign.
